# Stone Fort pilot scope and evidence log

Status: owner direction recorded September 30, 2026; feasibility is still open under [issue #1](https://github.com/Dyno-man/Crag-Commune/issues/1).

## Decisions recorded

- The desired pilot area is **all of Stone Fort**, rather than a preselected sector. This is the coverage goal, not a claim that every climb or path is ready to publish.
- A member with a profile may share their own climbing experience. Label it as **personal experience**, show who reported it and when, and keep it separate from reviewed catalog facts and official access notices. A member report cannot by itself open access, certify a grade, or create a navigable trail edge.
- The owner handles catalog corrections and community moderation. The product should give those actions a review queue and audit history when implemented.

## Coverage gate

Community outings may name Stone Fort using a reviewed public area name and current access link. Planner coverage is tracked separately. Activate each connected planning section only after approved climb data, current access review, source rights, and two independent local checks of its published paths and stop identification. Show unavailable sections as such. Never route between sections until the connecting edges have also been reviewed. This staged activation is how the whole-crag goal can be pursued without implying unreviewed coverage is navigable.

The original 30-to-60-climb figure was an estimate for one sector and is not a whole-crag completion target. Size and timing for the whole-crag catalog require a data audit and local review. There is no approved climbing data in this repository yet.

## Interview guide

Interview five to eight local climbers, including people newer to Chattanooga. Ask each person, with permission to record only non-sensitive notes:

1. How do you currently find people for an outing, and where does that fail?
2. What would make a public outing feel welcoming and clear enough to join?
3. Which Stone Fort session scenarios and self-reported grade ranges should the pilot support first?
4. What information about access or a climb would you trust, and what would you want labeled as uncertain or personal experience?
5. Would a plan covering only reviewed sections be useful while other sections remain unavailable?
6. What would count as a successful first month, and what would make you stop using the site?

Summarize patterns and disagreements without publishing names, private contact details, or sensitive locations. Distinguish what participants said from product assumptions. Do not mark the interview requirement complete until the interviews have happened.

## Open evidence and decisions

- Interview findings, supported scenarios and grade ranges, and measurable pilot success criteria.
- Two actual local field reviewers and the first reviewed section or connected graph to activate.
- Rights and maintenance capacity for the desired whole-crag catalog; issue #2 tracks the source audit.
- The exact area meant by “St. Elmo” and the intended Crag Conditions URL, confirmed with its owner.
